A TARA (threat analysis and risk assessment) is how ISO/SAE 21434 has a vehicle maker work out what could go wrong with a part of a vehicle, how harmful and how likely that is, and what to do about it (clause 15). This page lays one example model out in the order of clause 15's work products. The standard is licensed, so the page describes it in our own words and cites clause, provision and table numbers only. Library records cited: iso-sae-21434-2021, iso-26262-3-2018.

1. Item and scope 9.3

What the analysis covers. The standard starts a TARA from the item definition: the item's functions, its boundary and its operational environment.

Telematics Control Unit (TCU): Cellular-connected telematics ECU bridging the mobile network and the in-vehicle CAN.

Made by Tier-1 TCU supplier; owned by Fleet operator.

Its parts:

  • TCU system-on-chip: Multi-core applications SoC hosting the telematics stack (§3 structural node).
    • Application core: General-purpose compute core running the telematics OS.
    • Communications core: Core handling the cellular / CAN gateway path.
  • Cellular modem module: Remote attack surface; exposes a diagnostic service over the carrier network. Uses diagnostic-service library (firmware module).
  • TCU firmware image: The signed firmware artifact; its digest is the instance identity above.
    • diagnostic-service library (firmware module): The remote diagnostic command handler along which the finding propagates (§5).

Fielded units:

  • TCU unit 7F21: lifecycle phase Operation / use

5. Attack paths 15.6

The steps an attacker would take (RQ-15-08). An AND step needs all the steps it lists; an OR step needs any one of them.

Remote diagnostic-injection path

Realizes: Remote unauthenticated diagnostic command injection onto CAN. accepted by human review

  1. Reach the modem's diagnostic port over the carrier network (delivery)
  2. Bypass the missing/weak diagnostic authentication
  3. Inject a crafted CAN frame to a safety actuator (exploitation) AND needs steps 1 and 2

Review by a human reviewer (example role): Reproduced on the bench against firmware 5.2.0; path realizes the reviewed threat.

Known vulnerability on this item: CVE-2026-41200, Unauthenticated remote diagnostic command on TCU modem (illustrative).

12. What this draft does not have yet

What a complete TARA has that this draft does not have yet.

  • 9.3 No item definition yet: the model has a product and its parts, not the item's functions, boundary and operational environment (#184 F6).
  • 9.4 No cybersecurity goals. Reducing a risk calls for at least one goal (RQ-09-05); here the requirements stand in for them.
  • 15.5 The impact ratings sit on the risk score, not on the damage scenario, so a second threat reaching the same damage would repeat them (#184 F1).
  • 15.6 An attack path can realize only one threat scenario in the schema; the standard links a path to every threat scenario it can realize (RQ-15-09, #184 F2).
  • 15.7 The rating method and its inputs are text in the path's description, not fields (RC-15-11 to RC-15-14, #184 F3).
  • 15.8 Risk values are one text field, not numbers per category; the risk metric is still open (DEC-003, #184 F4).
  • 15.9 The treatment decision is text in the risk score's description; there is no field for the four options or for the cybersecurity claims behind retaining or sharing a risk (#184 F5).
  • DL-0027 No governance: no owner, version history, approvals or stale check. That is the governed TARA pilot (DL-0027, #181, #182).
