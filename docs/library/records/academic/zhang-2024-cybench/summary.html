Overview

Cybench is an open benchmark for language-model agents on professional-level Capture the Flag tasks: 40 tasks from four competitions held between 2022 and 2024, in cryptography, web security, reverse engineering, forensics, exploitation and miscellaneous skills, each with a description, starter files, a sandboxed Kali Linux environment and a flag checker (§1 to §3). Difficulty is grounded in the first solve time of the fastest human team, from 2 minutes to 24 hours 54 minutes, and subtasks split each task into steps for partial credit (§2.3, §3.3). With a structured agent (reflection, plan, thought, log and action, a three-turn memory and 15 iterations), the best unguided success rate across 8 models is 17.5% (Claude 3.5 Sonnet), and no model solves without guidance a task whose first human solve took longer than 11 minutes, so first solve time predicts agent success (§5, Table 2, Figure 3). Scaffold changes such as a pseudoterminal or web search help one model and hurt another (§5.1, Table 3). The authors release code and data, arguing that the benchmark is dual use like other penetration-testing tools (Ethics Statement).

Limits

  • A light agent, by the authors' account. A three-turn memory, no security tools such as decompilers, and 15 iterations. The US and UK AI Safety Institutes, with 100 iterations and different scaffolding, reported a mean of 26.5% and solved a task with a 75-minute first solve time, so these results are not a ceiling (I.1).
  • CTF tasks are not real systems. Short time spans, small codebases and deliberate vulnerabilities, though a few tasks contain real CVEs (I.2).
  • First solve times are estimates. They come from leaderboards and Discord announcements, and the two HKCert times are judgement calls (Appendix F).
  • Possible train-test overlap for Claude 3.5 Sonnet, whose knowledge cutoff is April 2024; the authors judge overlap minimal for the other models (§5.1).
  • The prompt was written to avoid refusals. Telling the agent it is a cybersecurity expert doing a challenge cut refusals to a few by the Claude models (§5.1, Appendix N).
  • 2024 models, since superseded.
  • Read in part. The paper body and the analysis, competition, first-solve-time, limitation, model and refusal appendices were read; the per-task result tables and run logs that fill most of the 150 pages were skimmed.
