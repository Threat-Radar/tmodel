Overview

Defines Trike, a threat-modeling method for security auditing from a risk and defender's perspective, distinguished by its formalism and high degree of automation. It builds a requirements model — actors, assets, intended actions (create/read/update/delete), and rules — summarised in an actor–asset–action matrix; then an implementation model (data-flow diagrams and "use flows"). Threats are generated deterministically from the matrix and fall into just two kinds: denial of service (an actor is prevented from an intended action) and elevation of privilege (an actor does something not allowed, or breaks an action's rules, or uses the system against another system). It argues spoofing, tampering and information disclosure are really attacks or kinds of elevation of privilege. Each threat becomes the root of an attack tree, and the trees join into one attack graph; it then separates weaknesses, vulnerabilities and mitigations, keeps reusable attack libraries, and adds a risk model using asset values, actor risk ratings and probabilities.

Limits

  • A draft. The document says parts are experimental and untested on real systems, and promises a worked example it does not include.
  • Superseded but not replaced. The project site describes v1.5 (which drops threat trees) and a v2 that is "yet-to-be-documented"; the site repository was last changed in 2019, so the project looks dormant despite saying it is under active development.
  • Vague scales. SEI (sei-threat-modeling-methods-2018) calls its five-point risk scales too vague for a formal method.
  • Start date is inconsistent. The site says the project began in 2006, but this document is dated 2005 and copyrighted 2003–2005.
  • Access. octotrike.org refuses automated access; the digest is of the Internet Archive copy.
