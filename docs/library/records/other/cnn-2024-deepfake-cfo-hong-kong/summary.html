Overview

CNN reports what Hong Kong police said at a briefing on Friday 2 February 2024. A finance worker at a multinational firm received a message, purportedly from the company's UK-based chief financial officer, about a secret transaction, and at first suspected phishing. He set his doubts aside after a video conference in which the other participants looked and sounded like colleagues he knew; according to Senior Superintendent Baron Chan Shun-ching, every one of them was a deepfake. He remitted a total of HK$200 million, about US$25.6 million, and the fraud came to light only when he later checked with head office. The police named neither the company nor the worker. At the same briefing they reported six arrests linked to such scams and, separately, that eight stolen Hong Kong identity cards were used for 90 loan applications and 54 bank-account registrations between July and September 2023, with AI deepfakes fooling facial recognition at least 20 times by imitating the card holders. The story closes with wider concern about deepfakes, citing the AI-generated explicit images of Taylor Swift that spread in late January 2024.
